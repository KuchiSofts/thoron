# Memory soak test, 8 hours (2026-11-16)

ROADMAP **Q.4**. `tools/qa/soak.py` on the Windows 158 release build (158.0.8127.3010, AVX2), offline, a
throwaway profile, on one Windows 11 x64 PC that was in use the whole time (builds and other tests ran beside
it; the measure is the private memory of the browser's own processes, which those do not change).

    Soak: 8.00 h, 4168 rounds of 6 tabs, 25008 pages opened, 24967 finished their work, 97 settled samples
    Private memory of the browser's process tree, in MB:
      with the tabs open: at most 1170.6
      settled: first sample 281.7, median of the second half 341.5
      trend over the second half (4.00 h, 49 samples): +2.11 MB per hour, +8.4 MB in all
      scatter of the settled samples 5.69, noise floor 17.1
      trend over the final quarter: +3.66 MB per hour
      trend by process type, MB per hour: browser +1.20, gpu +1.01, renderer -0.07, utility +0.01, other +0.00
    Limit: 10 MB per hour, above the noise floor, still climbing in the final quarter
    SOAK: PASSED

The settled memory rises from 282 MB to about 340 MB in the first two hours (caches and the GPU process
filling) and then stays level: +8.4 MB over the last four hours, half of the noise floor. The run confirms the
limit of 10 MB per hour: a real leak of that size would show as 40 MB over the second half, well above the
noise. 41 of the 25008 pages did not finish their work in time (a busy PC); none of the processes ended and no
crash dump was written.

`release.py prepare --soak <report>` refuses a release without a passing run of at least 8 hours of the
same build (the report names it, `Build:`).
