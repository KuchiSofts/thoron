# Video codecs on NVIDIA and AMD graphics: Thoron 158 and Chrome 157 (2026-11-17)

ROADMAP **P5.7**: upstream #1271 (HEVC and AV1 that play black, audio on, since Thorium 151) and #1285 (frames
dropped with hardware acceleration on NVIDIA graphics, smooth on integrated graphics). `tools/qa/codec_check.py`
on a Windows 11 PC that has both an NVIDIA discrete GPU (the one the displays are on, and the one the browser
picks) and AMD integrated graphics. Each clip is 8 s of ffmpeg's moving test pattern at 60 frames a second
(`--make-media`), played in a real window with hardware acceleration on, from a local server; counted are the
frames shown and dropped from the second second to the seventh (about 300), so that neither the start nor the
jump back of the loop is in it. Thoron 158 (158.0.8127.3010, AVX2) against Chrome 157 (157.0.8103.79) on the same
PC, one after the other; the 48-hour soak was running beside them the whole time.

## What it shows

- **#1271 is not there in Thoron 158:** every clip, HEVC 8 and 10 bit and AV1 included, was decoded by the
  graphics card itself (`D3D11VideoDecoder`, a platform decoder, in every run)
  and showed its picture (the darkest frame read had a brightness of 127 of 255; black is 0).
- **#1285 is not there in Thoron 158 on NVIDIA:** in three runs each, Thoron and Chrome dropped 0 to 6 of about
  300 frames per clip, mostly 0 to 2, at 4K60 in H.264, HEVC, AV1 and VP9 alike. Thoron dropped a few more in
  all: 29 of about 9,000 frames against Chrome's 17 (0.3 % against 0.2 %), most of them in its first run; one
  run differs from the next by as much (each browser had one clip with 6). A run on a quiet PC would tell
  whether the small difference is real.
- **The AMD integrated graphics drop frames in both browsers** (Thoron somewhat more in its one run) when the
  browser is made to use it
  (`--use-adapter-luid`) while the displays are on the NVIDIA card: every frame then has to cross from one
  adapter to the other. That is not the report's situation (an integrated GPU driving its own display), so it
  says nothing for or against #1285; a PC whose display is on AMD or Intel graphics is still to be measured.
